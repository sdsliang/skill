# Full-paragraph examples

These examples are closed edits: the rewrite uses only information present in the corresponding BEFORE paragraph. Numbers, identifiers, qualifiers, caveats, and completion status are preserved exactly. The examples show what to remove from report prose, not what to remove from the underlying evidence.

## Example 1: Phase 3 efficacy readout

**BEFORE**

> To provide a more reliable assessment of pembrolizumab in first-line metastatic NSCLC, we searched 3 databases and screened 42 records before extracting the efficacy data from the remaining reports. As shown in Table 1, the pembrolizumab arm achieved a median PFS of 9.4 months versus 6.1 months for chemotherapy, with HR 0.62 (95% CI 0.48-0.80) and p<0.001; median OS was 22.0 months versus 18.1 months, with HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.58-0.94). These results represent a pivotal breakthrough and showcase the robust clinical value of the regimen. Leading experts have stated that the data establish pembrolizumab as the definitive first-line standard. It is important to note that the OS analysis was not mature at the interim cutoff, and the follow-up duration was not identical across all records.

**AFTER**

> In first-line metastatic NSCLC, pembrolizumab improved PFS versus chemotherapy: median PFS was 9.4 months versus 6.1 months, with HR 0.62 (95% CI 0.48-0.80) and p<0.001. Median OS was 22.0 months versus 18.1 months, with HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.58-0.94). The OS analysis was not mature at the interim cutoff, and the follow-up duration was not identical across all records.

The opening search and screening account was deleted because it describes the writer's work rather than the clinical finding. The table restatement was compressed into the efficacy judgment and its supporting numbers; the table can carry the full display elsewhere. “Pivotal breakthrough,” “robust clinical value,” and the unnamed experts' conclusion were deleted because they add inflation and borrowed authority without a named source. The interim maturity and follow-up caveat stayed because they change how the OS result should be interpreted.

**Must survive verbatim:** `pembrolizumab`; `first-line metastatic NSCLC`; `9.4 months`; `6.1 months`; `HR 0.62`; `(95% CI 0.48-0.80)`; `p<0.001`; `22.0 months`; `18.1 months`; `HR 0.74`; `(95% CI 0.58-0.94)`; `not mature at the interim cutoff`; `the follow-up duration was not identical across all records`.

## Example 2: Pipeline status and planned study

**BEFORE**

> To map the current development landscape of adagrasib, we reviewed the company pipeline, registry entries, and the latest investor materials, then cross-checked each record against Table 2. The table shows 2 active registrational programs, including the planned Phase 3 KRYSTAL-12 study in colorectal cancer, and 1 Phase 2 combination study. The pipeline is not merely expanding; it is laying the foundation for a broad precision-oncology platform and represents a major step forward for KRAS G12C treatment. Industry analysts have called the program a likely category leader. As of the data cutoff of 2025-06-30, KRYSTAL-12 was planned but not yet initiated, and the registry did not report a planned start date. The combination study had 86 enrolled patients, of whom 31 had received prior anti-EGFR therapy.

**AFTER**

> As of the data cutoff of 2025-06-30, adagrasib had 2 active registrational programs and 1 Phase 2 combination study. KRYSTAL-12 was a planned Phase 3 study in colorectal cancer and remained planned but not yet initiated; the registry did not report a planned start date. The combination study had 86 enrolled patients, including 31 who had received prior anti-EGFR therapy.

The pipeline and registry review was deleted because the report should state the resulting status, not recount how the status was assembled. The table's row-by-row wording was replaced by the status summary; the study names and counts remain. “Broad precision-oncology platform,” “major step forward,” and the unnamed analysts' prediction were deleted because they inflate a pipeline description and provide no attributable evidence. “Planned but not yet initiated” and “did not report a planned start date” were retained separately: planned != initiated, and not reported != absent. The prior-treatment subgroup was retained because it defines the enrolled population rather than decorating the conclusion.

**Must survive verbatim:** `adagrasib`; `2 active registrational programs`; `1 Phase 2 combination study`; `KRYSTAL-12`; `planned Phase 3`; `colorectal cancer`; `2025-06-30`; `planned but not yet initiated`; `the registry did not report a planned start date`; `86 enrolled patients`; `31`; `prior anti-EGFR therapy`.

## Example 3: Safety and market access evidence

**BEFORE**

> For completeness, we searched 5 public sources and reconciled the safety tables before presenting the following overview. As reported in Table 3, grade >=3 treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 18% of patients receiving trastuzumab deruxtecan compared with 12% in the comparator arm, treatment discontinuation due to adverse events occurred in 6% versus 3%, and interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis occurred in 15% versus 2%. The safety profile is therefore highly manageable and highlights the product's differentiated value proposition. Several publications and market commentators have described trastuzumab deruxtecan as a compelling new standard. However, the analysis was based on a safety population of 204 patients and 198 patients, respectively, and the source did not specify whether the 15% and 2% interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis rates used the same ascertainment criteria. No pricing conclusion can be drawn because US net price was not reported.

**AFTER**

> In the safety population, grade >=3 treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 18% of patients receiving trastuzumab deruxtecan versus 12% in the comparator arm. Treatment discontinuation due to adverse events occurred in 6% versus 3%; interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis occurred in 15% versus 2%. The safety population included 204 patients and 198 patients, respectively. The source did not specify whether the 15% and 2% interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis rates used the same ascertainment criteria. US net price was not reported, so no pricing conclusion can be drawn.

The source-search and reconciliation narrative was deleted because it reports the analyst's process. The table restatement was reorganized around the safety findings and kept the exact rates. “Highly manageable,” “differentiated value proposition,” and the unnamed publications and commentators were deleted because the paragraph does not provide evidence for those evaluations. The population sizes and the ascertainment caveat were retained because they limit interpretation of the safety comparison. The final sentence preserves the distinction between a missing price disclosure and an absent price: not reported != absent.

**Must survive verbatim:** `trastuzumab deruxtecan`; `grade >=3 treatment-emergent adverse events`; `18%`; `12%`; `treatment discontinuation due to adverse events`; `6%`; `3%`; `interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis`; `15%`; `2%`; `204 patients`; `198 patients`; `the source did not specify whether the 15% and 2% interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis rates used the same ascertainment criteria`; `US net price was not reported`.

## Should be kept (do not touch)

- Genuine data cut-off and inclusion criteria statements, such as `As of the data cutoff of 2025-06-30` and a defined safety population.
- Genuine limitations, including an immature OS analysis, unequal follow-up, missing planned start dates, and unmatched adverse-event ascertainment criteria.
- A real authorial judgment when its evidence is present, such as a conclusion about comparative PFS supported by the reported HR, confidence interval, and p value.
- Legitimate formal register, including nominalization, passive voice where the actor is not material, and precise qualifiers such as `not yet initiated`, `not mature`, and `did not specify`.
- Quotations and proper nouns, including `KRYSTAL-12`, `pembrolizumab`, `adagrasib`, `trastuzumab deruxtecan`, and verbatim quoted source language.
